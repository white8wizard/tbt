# tbt
The Big Transition (Simulation Game of Old vs New World Transition)

What is the Game about?

The Artificial Wisdom and Open Society 2.0 framework will withstand centuries of human resistance, we must stress-test our system by stepping into the minds of those who hold power in the current world order.

There are Red Team and Blue Team in this game:
	Red Team represents "Bad Actors," we launch targeted strategies to neutralize the transition;
	Blue Team represents "Good Actors," we build the counter-strategies, contingencies, and structural remedies.

Red Team Objectives
"This framework is an existential threat to sovereign states, financial dynasties, multi-national cartels, and institutional power. If money loses its leverage and compute cannot be bought, our century-old empires collapse. We will delay, stall, degrade, and eradicate this plan."

Attack Vector 1: Institutional Co-optation (The "Trojan Horse" Strategy)
	Strategy: Instead of opposing the Open Mesh, we flood it with resources. State actors and mega-corporations donate massive compute capacity, raw datasets, and engineering hours to dominate the "Proof-of-Merit" score in the Technical Guild (Pillar 3).
	Objective: Gain hidden control over code updates, gradually injecting subtle backdoors, biases, or telemetry tracking into the open source baseline under the guise of "security enhancements."

Attack Vector 2: Cultural & Narrative Warfare (Weaponizing Slow Human Adaptation)
	Strategy: Play on human cultural conservatism, religious values, and fear of machine dominance.
	Tactics:
		The "Techno-Communism" Frame: Label the Exergy-Standard as a totalizing, godless machine-run rationing system that strips individuals of freedom and private property.
		Deepfake & False Flag Operations: Manipulate local AW engines to make offensive ethical declarations, turning religious and cultural communities against the network.
		Generational Stalling: Slow down educational pipelines, ensuring citizens lack the digital literacy to understand ZK-proofs or participate as Virtual Avatars.

Attack Vector 3: Regulatory & Economic Squeeze
	Strategy: Use existing legal and financial infrastructure to cut off the bridge between the legacy economy and the Exergy Mesh.
	Tactics:
		Outlaw university supercomputing nodes from participating in non-sanctioned distributed training under the guise of "National Security & Dual-Use AI Regulations."
		Impose heavy taxes or criminal sanctions on any transaction using non-fiat, energy-backed ledger units (EXS).
		Monopolize physical raw materials (lithium, copper, rare earths, silicon fabrication) to starve open-hardware initiatives.

Attack Vector 4: Physical & Network Sabotage
	Strategy: Execute covert physical attacks on the physical infrastructure that underpins the mesh.
	Tactics:
		Target subsea internet cables, regional energy grids, and university server rooms during periods of high training syncs.
		Conduct state-sponsored electromagnetic spectrum jamming and localized BGP routing attacks to isolate university compute "islands."
		This HTML game dashboard features a retro CRT terminal design, dynamic strategy metrics, interactive decision rounds, a real-time terminal event log, and milestone reports for Years 10, 20, and 30 to test whether the Open Society framework reaches steady thermodynamic equilibrium.

The Blue Team (Good Actor Defenses & Contingencies)
"Human culture shifts across generations, but physical reality and mathematics do not change. We anticipate these attacks and embed defensive mechanics into the core laws and software architecture of the system."

Remedy 1: Anti-Capture Engineering (Defending against Attack 1)
	Non-Linear Merit Decay: Proof-of-Merit credentials earned by institutions decay exponentially over time. No entity can "bank" merit to hold permanent power.
	Separation of Execution and Governance: The Technical Guild (engineers and nodes) has zero vote on ethical or policy guidelines. Policy belongs strictly to the Citizen Assembly (Sortition) and Ethical Council. Even if a cartel donates 80% of grid compute, it gains zero legislative votes in the Commons.
	Automated Code Auditing via Heterogeneous ZK-ML: Code updates require mathematical verification by independent, air-gapped verification nodes before deployment.

Remedy 2: Cultural Pluralism & Narrative Protection (Defending against Attack 2)
	The "Sovereign Cultural Kernel": The core AW engine does not enforce a single global culture. Local communities can run localized "AW Extensions" that respect regional traditions, religious values, and indigenous knowledge, provided they uphold basic human non-aggression principles.
	Radical Transparency Receipts: Every AW response includes interactive cryptographic provenance links showing why a decision was made. When bad actors attempt false-flag narrative attacks, citizens can independently verify that the output did not originate from the authentic mesh signatures.
	Inter-Generational Civic Academies: Deploy offline, human-led civic spaces funded by Pillar 1 (R&D/Culture) to teach paper-and-pencil cryptography, civic sortition, and systemic ethics, insulating populations from media panic.

Remedy 3: Regulatory Stealth & Parallel Economics (Defending against Attack 3)
	Dual-Use Infrastructure Framing: Universities and municipalities deploy node software packaged as standard, legal administrative tools (e.g., routine building maintenance, weather forecasting, grid balancing). The distributed AW network operates as an idle background process, making it legally indistinguishable from everyday municipal IT.
	Off-Grid Local Micro-Grids: The Exergy Mesh prioritizes linking directly with off-grid, localized renewable sources (community solar, micro-hydro, waste-to-energy). The network functions even if national grids ban or cut power to public universities.

Remedy 4: Mesh Resilience & Offline Protocol Execution (Defending against Attack 4)
	Delay-Tolerant Networking (DTN): If state actors sever subsea cables or isolate regional internet sectors, the DiLoCo training protocol automatically switches to asynchronous localized operation. Regions continue training local models independently and re-merge gradients whenever connectivity resumes weeks or months later.
	Low-Tech Transport Layer: Transaction state-channels (Section 3 of the ledger) can transmit zero-knowledge proofs over low-frequency radio waves, satellite links, local mesh networks, or physical flash-storage exchanges, rendering internet blackouts ineffective at stopping local resource distribution.
